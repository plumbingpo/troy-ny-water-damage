Crawl Space Water Removal: What Troy Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Warning Signs

  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy

How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Termites and Other Pests

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
