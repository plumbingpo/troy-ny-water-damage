Bathroom Water Damage Help in Troy

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help Troy homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

A Failed Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Fresh caulk keeps additional water out, and that is worth doing. The water that is already inside the wall or under the tile stays there.
