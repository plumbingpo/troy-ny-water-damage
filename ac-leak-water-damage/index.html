AC Leak Water Damage in Troy: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
