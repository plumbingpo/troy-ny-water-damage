Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Troy Homeowners Should Know

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. This takes longer than drying carpet or drywall, often a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Signs to Look For

  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • Board centers humped higher than the edges, called crowning
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Set Drying Mats

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Monitor and Advise

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Our final advice is one of three outcomes, no further work, swapping a few boards, or refinishing the floor.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Growth Beneath the Floor

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. After the moisture leaves, each board is left with a raised center, which is crowning.

Water That Keeps Moving

Once below the boards, water moves. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Cupping Starts

The edges come up as each board expands. Finish may turn cloudy. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.
