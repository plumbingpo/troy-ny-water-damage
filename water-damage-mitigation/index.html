Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. It is not the repair. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. Our Troy crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. In fact there are two separate stages. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
