Wet Carpet Drying Help in Troy

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Troy extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.
