Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Troy: What to Know

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In Troy, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. Clean water means extraction and drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A stuck flapper or fill valve that keeps the tank running without stopping.
  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.

What the Water Damages

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Lingering Odor

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

1

Determine the Water Type

We find out whether the water came from the supply side or the bowl and drain, then set up the right protection and containment before any cleanup begins.

2

Extract and Remove

We extract standing water, pull contaminated carpet pad, baseboards or drywall where needed, and lift the toilet if water has gone under the base and flange.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Every fixture, the floor, the subfloor and the base of each wall gets cleaned and receives an antimicrobial treatment. With Category 3 water this step is done before drying equipment is switched on.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.
