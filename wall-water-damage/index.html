Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Troy

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

What It Looks Like

  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.
