Restoration Challenges New York City Homeowners Face

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for New York City households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in New York City, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Troy Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.

Water Damage Insurance Claims in New York City

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing a New York City homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Hold damaged flooring and possessions until your adjuster has inspected them or said that pictures are enough. Keep receipts for anything you spend because of the loss, from fans to a hotel stay.
