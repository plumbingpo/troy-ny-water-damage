Kitchen Water Damage in Troy

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Troy crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
